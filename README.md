# MiniAppShield

**Telegram Mini App Intelligence**

MiniAppShield is a Telegram Mini App intelligence and audit platform focused on runtime analysis, advertising intelligence, infrastructure relationships, and historical monitoring.

## What is MiniAppShield?

Telegram Mini Apps are increasingly used for games, Web3 products, wallets, advertising funnels, commerce, loyalty systems, and other digital services.

A simple username, landing page, or static URL check does not provide enough context for commercial or technical review.

MiniAppShield is designed to help advertisers, agencies, ad networks, security teams, partners, and due-diligence teams understand what is actually observed inside a Telegram Mini App.

The platform combines structured technical observations with historical intelligence.

---

## Core Intelligence Layers

MiniAppShield separates different types of evidence instead of reducing everything to a single risk score.

### Runtime Activity and Liveness

MiniAppShield examines whether meaningful Mini App runtime behavior was observed during an audit.

Signals may include:

- application initialization
- rendered content
- HTTP response state
- Telegram WebView bridge activity
- network requests
- interactive surfaces
- runtime failures
- page-level errors
- console errors

---

### Advertising Intelligence

MiniAppShield distinguishes between different stages of advertising evidence.

Examples:

- advertising technology detected
- advertising configuration observed
- ad-related runtime execution
- creative delivery evidence
- impression-related evidence

Important distinction:

**Advertising SDK detected does not mean an advertisement was actually delivered.**

---

### Technical Infrastructure

Runtime and structural observations can include:

- contacted domains
- application endpoints
- backend services
- third-party SDKs
- analytics infrastructure
- advertising infrastructure
- JavaScript assets
- application-specific technical artifacts

These observations can later support comparison between Telegram Mini Apps.

---

### Technical Relationships

MiniAppShield can identify exact observed technical relationships between applications.

Technical overlap does not automatically prove common ownership, fraud, or malicious intent.

The system focuses on evidence-based relationship descriptions rather than unsupported attribution.

---

### Advertising Relationships

MiniAppShield can also identify exact advertising relationships based on observed advertising identity or infrastructure.

The goal is to help analysts understand whether multiple Mini Apps share the same observed advertising context.

---

### Historical Monitoring

A single scan shows what was observed once.

Historical monitoring can reveal what changed over time.

MiniAppShield is designed to track changes such as:

- destination changes
- infrastructure changes
- SDK changes
- advertising stack changes
- technical relationship changes
- runtime behavior changes
- Telegram audience metadata changes

MiniAppShield is building a structured historical intelligence dataset for the Telegram Mini App ecosystem.

The Observatory combines:

- public Mini App discovery
- source normalization
- duplicate removal
- Mini App validation
- runtime analysis
- relationship mapping
- historical observations
- change intelligence

The goal is not simply to collect Telegram usernames.

The goal is to build a clean and deeply analyzed historical intelligence layer for Telegram Mini Apps.

---

## Evidence Principles

MiniAppShield separates observed evidence from interpretation.

Examples:

- SDK detected ≠ ad delivery
- ad request ≠ confirmed impression
- technical relationship ≠ common ownership
- shared infrastructure ≠ fraud
- absence of observed advertising ≠ advertising is never present
- runtime degradation ≠ malicious behavior

This evidence-first approach is designed to make findings easier to review and explain.

## Why Historical Intelligence Matters

A Telegram Mini App may look normal during a single observation.

Historical analysis can provide additional context.

For example:

- the WebView destination may change
- new SDKs may appear
- advertising integrations may change
- new domains may be contacted
- technical relationships may emerge
- runtime behavior may change

None of these facts independently proves wrongdoing.

Together, however, they can provide useful context that a one-time inspection cannot provide.
